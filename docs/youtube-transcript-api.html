YouTube transcript API: what works in 2026

Getting the transcript of a public YouTube video sounds like one API call. In practice there are three routes, and two of them stop working in production for most people.

Your options

OptionAny public video?Works from a server?Notes
Official YouTube Data API (captions.download)No: it needs OAuth and permission to edit the videoYes200 quota units per download. It's meant for your own videos.
youtube-transcript-api (open-source Python library)YesOften blocked: its README says YouTube blocks most IPs of cloud providers such as AWS, Google Cloud and Azure, and recommends rotating residential proxiesGreat on a laptop. Budget for proxies in production.
Hosted transcript tools, e.g. on Apify StoreYesYes, proxies includedPay per transcript, no setup.

Five things to check in any transcript

  1. Manual or auto-generated? Auto captions come from speech recognition and often miss names, numbers and jargon. If you feed transcripts to an AI, label which is which.
  2. The right language? Some tools quietly fall back to another language when yours isn't available.
  3. Coverage. How much of the video has words on screen? A long video with a short transcript is missing pieces, or is mostly music.
  4. Sound tags. Lines like [Music] and (Applause) add noise to search and embeddings.
  5. Videos without captions. Some tools fail the whole run, others silently return nothing. You want a row that says why.

FAQ

Is there an official API for YouTube transcripts? Only captions.download, and only for videos you have permission to edit.

Why does youtube-transcript-api fail on my server? Per its README, YouTube blocks most IPs that belong to cloud providers. Rotating residential proxies are the workaround it recommends.

What does it cost? With our tool, $0.002 per transcript on Apify's free-plan pricing, less on paid plans. Videos without a transcript are free.
